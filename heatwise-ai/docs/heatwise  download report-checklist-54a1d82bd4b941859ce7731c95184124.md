# Heatwave preparedness checklist

## Households

### Before the heat
- [ ] Keep the home cooler by covering windows with curtains or shades, and find nearby cool public places you can go to during the hottest days.
- [ ] Keep drinking water within easy reach of everyone at home so people can drink regularly on hot days.
- [ ] Note the older people, young children, pregnant women and people with chronic illness nearby whom you will check on during hot days.
- [ ] Learn the signs of heat illness, such as heavy sweating, dizziness, confusion, fainting or hot dry skin, and know how to contact local emergency services or the nearest health facility.
- [ ] Follow weather forecasts and any official heat warnings issued by local authorities.

### During the heat
- [ ] Drink water regularly through the day.
- [ ] Avoid heavy activity during the hottest part of the day and rest in the coolest place available.
- [ ] If you need to go outside, stay in the shade and wear a wide hat and loose, light-coloured clothing.
- [ ] Take cool showers or baths and cook less with the oven; do not rely on a fan as your main way to cool down.
- [ ] Check regularly on older family members, neighbours and young children, and never leave children or pets in a parked vehicle.
- [ ] If someone is confused, faints or has very hot, dry skin, contact local emergency services or the nearest health facility immediately, move them to a cool shaded place and cool them with wet cloths.

### After the heat
- [ ] Keep drinking water regularly while it stays hot, and refill stored drinking water for the next hot spell.
- [ ] Rest and recover in a cool place; if cramps or tiredness from the heat appear, move somewhere cooler, remove extra clothing and sip water.
- [ ] If heat cramps or exhaustion get worse or last more than an hour, contact a health provider or the nearest health facility.
- [ ] Keep checking on vulnerable neighbours and relatives until the heat has passed.
